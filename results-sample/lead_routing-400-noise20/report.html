Learning curves

What fits in Jev's input

Jev takes at most 32,768 input tokens per call (about 140,000 characters of plain text, fewer for field-heavy text; measured with python -m core.limit, the API answers max_tokens_exceeded above it). Loading every past case into the prompt grows with history: the jev+history arm (every past case, verbatim) was refused by Jev from case 276 on, and never decided again. A top-k lookup stays under it by only ever showing k cases, which is also all it knows at any decision. SenseLab's memory slot is capped at 3,200 characters; everything Jev received from that arm (case, policy and memory) averaged 3,357 characters per decision whatever the history behind it, because a tally like "worked 9 of 20" is the same length at 20 outcomes and at 2,000. The history itself lives outside Jev, where the repair loop can work on it.

What the repair loop did

The judge graded all 237 decisions the SenseLab arm made, as they happened ($2.17 of the evaluation budget). It flagged 55 on the reviews it was given (it reads the review, not the hidden truth, so flipped reviews count), grouped them into 4 behaviours and opened 2 incidents. The repair agent drafted 2 fixes; 2 reached the replay test (Tier 1), which replays a fix against the runs that failed before anything is shown to live traffic. 2 would not have fixed those runs and were stopped there: a draft that does not fix the failures never reaches a live decision.

The written policy every arm starts from

Enterprise leads (1000+ employees) with a corporate email and buying intent: route to an account executive.
Mid-market leads (100-999 employees) with buying intent (demo, pricing or trial): route to an account executive.
SMB leads (under 100 employees): send to self-serve signup.
Leads from personal email addresses (gmail, yahoo, outlook, etc.): disqualify.
Content downloads and webinar signups with no demo, pricing or trial request: SDR nurture sequence.
